🏆 Big Picture Words

The basics you need before anything else makes sense.

Stock
A tiny slice of a real company that you can own. If a company is a giant pizza, one stock is one slice of it.
Ticker e.g. NVX
A short nickname for a stock, made of letters, so people don't have to type the whole company name every time.
Investing
Buying a small piece of a company because you think it will grow and become worth more later — like planting a seed and waiting for it to grow into a tree.
Portfolio
The whole collection of stocks (and other things) someone owns — like a backpack full of all your trading cards, not just one.
Scanner
The tool on the main page that checks all 63 companies at once and lines them up from "most interesting" to "least interesting."
Opportunity Score
A single number from 0–100 that sums up how interesting a stock looks right now, based on everything the app knows about it.
Think of it like a report card grade, but for a stock instead of a student.

⭐ The 7 Scores

Every stock gets graded on these seven things — they add up to the Opportunity Score.

Quality
How well-run and financially healthy the company is — does it make good decisions and manage its money responsibly?
Growth
How much bigger the company's sales and profits are getting over time.
Like measuring how much taller a kid has grown since last year.
Momentum
How fast the stock's price has been moving lately, and in which direction.
A ball rolling downhill keeps speeding up — momentum measures if the stock's price is "rolling" up or down right now.
Value
Whether the stock's price looks cheap or expensive compared to how much the company is actually worth.
Like noticing a toy is on sale for way less than it's normally worth.
Cash Flow
How much real cash is actually flowing into the company's bank account — not just money it's owed, but money it truly has.
Catalyst
A recent event — like a big announcement or great earnings report — that could cause the stock price to move.
Like a spark that could start a fire.
Risk
How bumpy or dangerous the ride might be. A high risk score means the stock could swing wildly up or down.
The difference between a gentle bike ride and a rollercoaster.
Signal Convergence
How many different clues (out of the app's 10 strategies) all agree that a stock is interesting right now.
If ten friends all independently tell you the same rumor, you start to believe it more than if just one friend said it.

🎯 The 10 Strategies

Each strategy is a different "recipe" for spotting a specific kind of opportunity.

🟢 Great Company at a Good Price
Looks for strong, healthy companies that happen to be selling for less than they're really worth.
🔥 Momentum Breakout Finder
Looks for stocks that just broke out of their normal price range with a lot of people buying — a real trend, not just a random blip.
🚀 Earnings Momentum
Looks for companies that just reported better profits than expected, and where things keep getting better.
🟠 Fallen Angel
Looks for good companies whose stock price dropped a lot, but the company itself is still healthy — a possible comeback story.
⭐ Undervalued Quality
Looks for companies that are high-quality, growing, financially strong, AND cheap — all at the same time (the whole package).
🏦 Institutional Accumulation
Looks for signs that big investors (like banks and pension funds) are quietly buying up shares of a company.
Like noticing all the popular kids slowly starting to like the same new game.
🟢 Insider Signal
Looks for cases where the people who actually work at a company (who know it best) are buying its stock themselves.
🔥 Short Squeeze Watch
Looks for stocks where a lot of people bet the price would go DOWN, but it's actually going UP — which can force them to buy back in and push the price up even more.
🟠 Mean Reversion
Looks for healthy stocks that dropped a bit too fast and are likely to "bounce back" toward normal.
Like a stretched rubber band snapping back toward its normal shape.
Mode: Investor / Swing Trader / Active Trader
How long you'd expect to hold onto a stock: Investor = years, Swing Trader = weeks, Active Trader = days or even hours.

💰 Money & Company Words

Words about how much a company is worth and how it makes money.

Market Cap
The total value of every single slice (share) of the company added up — basically, "how big is this company worth, in total?"
Revenue
All the money a company brings in from selling its stuff, before paying any of its own bills.
If you sold lemonade for $50, your revenue is $50 — even before you pay for the lemons.
EPS (Earnings Per Share)
How much profit the company made, divided evenly across every single share — like splitting a pizza's leftover slices evenly among everyone who owns a piece.
P/E Ratio
Compares a stock's price to how much profit the company makes per share. A high number means people are paying a lot for each dollar of profit.
Like asking, "how many years of allowance would it take to buy this?"
Forward P/E
The same idea as P/E, but using how much profit the company is expected to make next year instead of this year.
PEG Ratio
A version of P/E that also considers how fast the company is growing — it helps tell "expensive but growing fast" apart from "expensive and barely growing."
EV/EBITDA
Another way to check if a company is priced cheaply or expensively, comparing its full value (including debts) to its core profits.
Free Cash Flow (FCF)
The cash a company has left over after paying for everything it needs to keep running — real, spare cash it could use however it wants.
Fair Value
The app's best estimate of what a stock SHOULD be worth, based on its profits and growth — which you can compare to what it's actually trading for.
ROIC / ROE
Measures how good a company is at turning money it has into MORE money. Higher usually means it's run efficiently.
Like comparing two kids who both started with $10 — whoever turned it into $20 the fastest is better at "growing" their money.
Gross Margin / Operating Margin
How much profit is left over from each dollar of sales, after paying different kinds of costs. Higher margins mean the company keeps more of what it earns.
Leverage (Debt)
How much borrowed money a company is using compared to its own cash. Too much debt can be risky if things go wrong.

📈 Chart & Trading Words

Words about how a stock's price has been moving.

RSI (14)
A number from 0–100 that shows if a stock has been bought SO much lately that it might be "overheated," or sold so much it might be "too cold."
Kind of like a speedometer for buying and selling — it warns you when things are moving too fast in one direction.
Moving Average (20/50/200-day)
The average price of a stock over the last 20, 50, or 200 days — it smooths out the noisy day-to-day wiggles so you can see the real trend.
Volume
How many shares of a stock were bought and sold on a given day — basically, how busy the trading was.
52-Week High / Low
The highest and lowest prices a stock has hit over the last full year.
Relative Strength
Compares how a stock has done against the overall market — is it winning the race or falling behind everyone else?
Volatility
How much a stock's price jumps around, up and down. Higher volatility = a bumpier, less predictable ride.
Short Interest
How many people have bet that a stock's price will go DOWN. A high number means a lot of people are betting against it.
Days to Cover
If everyone who bet the price would go down had to change their mind at once, roughly how many days of normal trading it would take for them all to buy back in.
Institutional Ownership
What percentage of a company is owned by big, professional investors (like banks and mutual funds) instead of everyday individual people.
Insider Buying/Selling
When people who work at the company (who know it best) buy or sell its stock themselves.
Guidance
What a company's own leaders predict will happen to profits or sales in the near future.

🧪 Backtesting Words

Backtesting is like a time machine — testing whether a strategy would have actually worked in the past.

Backtest
Replaying history to see how a strategy would have performed if you had actually used it, months or years ago.
Point-in-Time
Only using information that was ACTUALLY known on that specific day in the past — not peeking at things that were discovered later.
Like taking a test with only the notes you had before the test — not the answer key you found afterward.
Look-Ahead Bias
A mistake where a backtest accidentally "cheats" by using information from the future — which would make a strategy look better than it really was.
Total Return
How much money you would have made (or lost) in total, from start to finish.
Annualized Return
The total return converted into "per year" terms, so you can fairly compare strategies tested over different lengths of time.
Benchmark (S&P 500)
A well-known basket of 500 big companies used as the "average" to compare a strategy against — did you beat the average, or fall behind it?
Max Drawdown
The biggest drop from a peak to a low point during the whole test — basically, "what's the worst it ever got?"
Sharpe Ratio
A score that measures how much return you got compared to how bumpy the ride was. Higher is better — good returns without wild swings.
Sortino Ratio
Similar to the Sharpe Ratio, but it only cares about the bumpy DOWNWARD moves — it doesn't punish a strategy for going up quickly.
Win Rate
Out of all the trades made, what percentage of them actually made money.
Profit Factor
Compares all the money made from winning trades to all the money lost from losing trades. Above 1 means the winners outweighed the losers overall.
Turnover
How much of the portfolio gets swapped out for different stocks each time it's rebalanced.

🔐 Account & App Words

Words about using StockFind Pro itself, not about stocks.

Sign Up / Log In
Creating your own account (Sign Up) or entering your email and password to get back into one you already made (Log In).
Subscription
Paying regularly (like monthly) to keep using the full app, instead of paying once.
Admin Dashboard
A special, locked page that only the people running StockFind Pro can see — it shows how many people are using the app and what they're doing.
Activity Log
A running list of things that happened in the app — like who signed up, who looked at which stock, and when.
Simulated Data
Made-up (but realistic-feeling) numbers used for practicing with the app — the 63 companies in StockFind Pro aren't real companies!
